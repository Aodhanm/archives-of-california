The Governors of California, 1769 to 1850

Every governor under Spain and Mexico: his title, his dates, where he actually governed from, and what this archive holds on him.

California had some thirty governors between Portolá and statehood, and the list is harder than it looks. Several men held the office twice; several held only half of it, since the civil and military commands could be split between two rivals; one governed from San Diego for six years and another moved the capital to Los Ángeles. The table below gives each of them, and where this archive holds his correspondence the name links to his records. Citations in the form C-A 17 name the manuscript volume at the Bancroft Library.

Titles changed with the regime. Under Spain the office was gobernador político y militar, one man holding both the civil government and the military command. Under Mexico it became jefe político for the civil side and comandante general for the military, and those two halves were repeatedly held by different men, which is the root of most of the period's conflicts. See How California Was Governed for the charts.

Spanish governors of Las Californias, 1769 to 1804

One governor for the whole of Las Californias, seated at Loreto in Baja California until the Real Orden of 19 April 1776 moved the residence to Monterey (C-A 1, doc 85), effective 1777. Men marked comandante ran Alta California without holding the governorship itself.

Spanish governors of Alta California, 1804 to 1822

The King divided Las Californias in 1804 and the division took effect in 1806, Goycoechea being recognised at Loreto on 5 July 1806 (C-A 25, doc 570). Alta California now had its own governor at Monterey; Baja California kept Loreto. See the 1804 chart.

11Pablo Vicente de Solá1815–1822MontereyLast Spanish governor; oversaw the transition to Mexico. His outgoing letter-register survives as C-A 17.

Mexican governors and jefes políticos of Alta California, 1822 to 1846

Independence split the office in two: the civil jefe político and the military comandante general, often held by different men. An elected assembly, the Diputación, was installed at Monterey in 1822 and could hand the government to its senior member when the seat fell vacant.

United States military governors, 1846 to 1849

Conquest did not create a civil government at once. California was run by naval and army officers until the constitutional convention of 1849 and statehood in 1850.

Where this comes from. The dates and titles follow the standard accounts, chiefly Bancroft's History of California. What is added here, and what you cannot get from a textbook list, is the archival side: which governor's own letters survive, where he actually sat, and which document records each turning point. Citations in the form C-A 45, doc 55 are records in this catalogue; search the volume and document number on the main page to read the entry and reach the manuscript scan.
Seats of government are a subject in their own right, because the official capital and the place the governor actually worked were often different. The capitals chart sets the law against the practice, including the nine years when Los Ángeles was the capital by decree and every governor stayed at Monterey.
