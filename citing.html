How to cite a document from the Archives of California

The form the literature uses for BANC MSS C-A manuscripts, the page trap that produces wrong citations, and worked examples.

The Archives of California are cited by series name, tomo, and original page, with the C-A call number acting as the repository locator. The working references you see while searching a catalogue, including this one, are finding aids rather than citations: scan numbers, Savage page numbers, and document ids belong in your notes, not in a footnote.

The standard form

First, full citation [Author] to [Recipient], [place], [date], [Series], [tomo]:[original pages], Archives of California, C-A [N], Bancroft Library, University of California, Berkeley (hereafter [abbreviation]).
Short form thereafter Prov. St. Pap., XIX:1053-58.

This follows Bancroft's own practice, which is still the convention in California History and the monograph literature. Bancroft wrote, for example, Prov. St. Pap., MS., xix. 1053.

The page trap, which is where most citations go wrong

Cite the original tomo page, not the Savage page. Each leaf usually carries two numbers: a stamped number at the top right, which is the page of Thomas Savage's copy, and the original tomo page, usually footed on the leaf as "pp. 1053-8" or written in the margin. Bancroft cites the second. Citing the stamped number produces a reference that looks right and leads a reader nowhere.

Two honest complications. Bancroft's practice varied by series, so the safest habit is to verify the convention volume by volume rather than assume it. And where Savage wrote a summary leaf rather than a transcription, for instance an abstract of a muster roll, the original page can be genuinely ambiguous; in that case cite the Savage page and say so.

A worked example

Take Arrillaga's 1796 reconnaissance diary, catalogued here as C-A 8, document 148. The record gives you scan n90 to n96, Savage pages 94 to 100, and original pages 279 to 345. Only the last of those belongs in the footnote:

José Joaquín de Arrillaga, "Diario de los reconocimientos que verificó el teniente coronel que suscribe en la Frontera de la gentilidad de la antigua California y márgenes del Colorado," San Vicente, 9 December 1796, Prov. St. Pap., XIV:279-345, Archives of California, C-A 8, Bancroft Library, University of California, Berkeley.

Note what the catalogue adds and the footnote omits: the scan numbers let you open the manuscript leaf, and they belong in your working file so you can return to it.

What you are actually citing

The originals of the Archives of California burned in the 1906 San Francisco fire. What survives for most of this material is the copy Thomas Savage and his assistants made in the 1870s for Hubert Howe Bancroft, now at the Bancroft Library. A citation to C-A is therefore a citation to that copy, and careful work says so when the distinction matters, for instance when an argument turns on spelling, a struck word, or a signature that a copyist would not reproduce.

Verify quotations against the manuscript. This catalogue's summaries are finding aids. Before you quote or give an exact date, open the linked scan and read the leaf. The catalogue publishes its own errata register for the cases where it got something wrong.

Citing the catalogue itself

If you want to cite this index rather than a manuscript, for instance in a data-availability statement or a methods note:

Coyne, Aodhan. Archives of California: A Documentary Calendar of the Savage Transcripts (BANC MSS C-A). Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21327098

Individual records have stable permalinks of the form archivesofcalifornia.com/record/ca8/ca8-d148.html, which are durable and can be cited directly.

Citing the manuscript scan

The scans are hosted by the Internet Archive from the Bancroft Library's digitisation. Each record page links to the exact leaf. If you cite a scan, cite the manuscript in the normal way and add the scan as an access note, since the Internet Archive item is a surrogate rather than the source of authority.
